# Compact arcade art

The player requested smaller graphics than the controller-check ship. The playable ship is 26 × 16 pixels (the proof silhouette was about 44 × 24), leaving a broad visible playfield. Scouts are 18 × 14, fighters 24 × 18, gunships 36 × 26 and asteroids 24 × 24. The two-phase dreadnaught is 88 × 84, large enough to read as a boss without filling the screen.

Original pixel sprites are generated by scripts/make_art.py and retained as transparent PNGs under assets/source. The converter reuses the source-PNG-to-packed-runtime approach of Little Wilds, adapted to native-layout RGB332. Each byte indexes a 256-colour RGB565 palette. Index zero is transparent; opaque colours that quantise to zero are remapped. Columns are stored bottom-to-top, matching the rotated native framebuffer, so sprite blits walk contiguous memory. There is no PNG decoding or asset allocation during flight.

Enemy bullets use bright gold with white cores and dark-blue outlines, distinct from thin cyan player lasers. Parallax stars remain small and low contrast. Explosion effects use bounded, short-lived pixel fragments rather than large opaque flashes. The bomb expands two cyan sweep edges instead of flashing the full panel white.

To regenerate: install Pillow, then run `python3 starbreak/scripts/make_art.py`. Generated files are committed, so firmware compilation does not require Pillow. The UI glyph atlas is derived from DejaVu Sans; its licence is in assets/FONT-LICENSE.txt.

## Campaign additions

Original compact sprites: interceptor 22×16, mine 18×18, turret 28×24, Warden 70×64 and Hive Carrier 82×70; the final Dreadnaught retains the 88×84 boss sprite. All sources remain transparent PNGs generated by `scripts/make_art.py`, with native indexed binaries regenerated alongside them. A blinking mine core helps distinguish mines from ordinary rocks.

The renderer draws distant planets, scrolling crystal-canyon boundaries and angular station structures without allocating terrain objects. Shared boundary functions keep the visible terrain aligned with ship collision. The two-line footer shows pilot rank/experience and sector; at the maximum rank it reads ACE.
